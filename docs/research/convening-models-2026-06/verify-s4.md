# Adversarial fact-check: arXiv 2509.05396

**Source:** "Talk Isn't Always Cheap: Understanding Failure Modes in Multi-Agent Debate"
**Authors:** Andrea Wynn (JHU), Harsh Satija (Vector Institute), Gillian K. Hadfield (JHU). v2, 13 Oct 2025.
**Accessed:** HTML version at https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v2 (the PDF at /pdf/ is FlateDecode-compressed and yielded no extractable text; HTML is the authoritative full text). Abstract corroborated at /abs/2509.05396.
**Setup (load-bearing for scope):** 3 underlying LLMs — GPT-4o-mini (strong), Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B (weak). Tasks: MMLU, CommonSenseQA, GSM8K. 100 random samples, 5 seeds, mean +/- standard error. Baseline ("w/o Debate") = **majority vote over agents' initial responses** (no exchange of reasoning) — this exactly matches the claim's stated baseline.

## Findings

### Claim 1 — "Debate can systematically DEGRADE accuracy vs no-debate baseline; on CommonSenseQA debate ALWAYS harmed across configs; degradation not a rare edge case."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED (with one scoping nuance).**

- Degradation vs baseline: paper says debate "can sometimes degrade performance, leading to worse final answers than those generated by a single agent acting alone. **These failures are not rare edge cases, but arise systematically** in settings where agents amplify each other's errors." This is a near-verbatim match for "not a rare edge case."
- CommonSenseQA always harmed: Sec 5.1 — "in the case of CommonSenseQA, which was not studied in prior work on multi-agent debate (Du et al., 2023), **debate always harms performance.**" Direct, unhedged author statement.
- **Scoping nuance (not a refutation):** "debate always harms" is asserted over the *debate configurations tested* (the multi-agent rows of Table 1, all of which show a red down-arrow for CSQA), not literally "every conceivable config in the universe." The single-agent rows are baselines, not debate. The claim's phrase "ALWAYS harmed across configs" is faithful to the paper's own "always harms" given that reading. Caveat the reader should keep: this is on 100 samples x 5 seeds with GPT-4o-mini / Llama-3.1-8B / Mistral-7B only — "always" is empirical over this design, not a proof.

### Claim 2 — "Adding a weaker/less-capable agent to a debate with a stronger agent can hurt — worse than no debate — contradicting 'diversity of abilities improves debate.'"

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**

- Verbatim: "we discover that **introducing a weak or less capable (lower-performing) LLM agent into a debate with a strong or more capable (higher-performing) agent can detrimentally affect the debate outcome, producing results worse than if the agents had not engaged in discussion. The presence of a weaker agent disrupts the performance of the stronger agent.**"
- The "contradicting diversity helps" framing is the paper's explicit motivation: it positions itself "Contrary to the prevalent belief that LLM agents are inherently collaborative — suggesting that a mixture of diverse models improves response quality." Match.
- Note the hedge "**can** detrimentally affect" — it is a demonstrated failure mode, not a universal law. The claim's word "can hurt" correctly preserves this. No overstatement.

### Claim 3 — "Social pressure/conformity drives undesirable flips: correct->incorrect more often than incorrect->correct; likelihood of undesirable flip rises as more peers disagree (worst when correct agent isolated)."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**

- Correct->incorrect dominates: Abstract + Fig 2 — "of those that do change their answers, **more change from a correct answer to an incorrect one (red region) than from an incorrect to a correct one (green region).**" Match.
- Isolation effect: Sec 6.2 — "the **likelihood of an undesirable answer-flip is highest when the ego agent is isolated** – i.e. when no other agents agree with its answer – **and decreases as more peers agree.**" This is the exact monotone-in-peer-agreement relationship the claim states (equivalently, rises as more peers disagree). Match.
- Caveat the paper itself flags (reader should keep): "this effect is **highly variable** across different datasets and models" — e.g. on GSM8K the strongest and weakest models flip *less* than the middle (LLaMA). The paper calls this "**strong preliminary evidence**," not settled causation. The claim doesn't overclaim causality, so still SUPPORTED, but "preliminary" is the author's own calibration.

### Claim 4 — "Performance progressively deteriorates as debate continues for more rounds; longer debate is sometimes actively harmful."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED — but watch the quantifier; the paper hedges with 'may'/'can'/'in many cases', not a universal monotone decline.**

- "Progressively deteriorates": contribution list — "we show that the performance **may progressively deteriorate as the debate progresses.**" The claim's word "progressively" is the paper's own.
- "Longer debate actively harmful": "in certain cases, **the longer a debate continues, the more performance can degrade.** In other words: talk isn't always cheap – and in some cases, it's **actively harmful.**" The phrase "actively harmful" is verbatim.
- Round mechanism (Fig 3): "agents that can resist flipping originally correct answers in round 1 have lower resistance to the social pressure from disagreement after round 2" — supports the round-over-round worsening mechanism.
- **Why not OVERSTATED:** the claim says "sometimes actively harmful" and "progressively deteriorates," both matching the paper's hedged "may / can / in certain cases / in many cases." Had the claim said "always deteriorates every round," it would be OVERSTATED — Fig 1 is described as degradation "**in many cases**" / "**frequently**," not universally, and several configs do not monotonically decline. The claim's "sometimes" keeps it within bounds.

## Bottom line for the team

All four claims are **SUPPORTED** by the paper's own text, mostly with verbatim or near-verbatim phrasing. The only place to tighten language when citing:
1. "ALWAYS harmed across configs" for CommonSenseQA is the author's literal word ("debate always harms performance") but holds over the *debate configs tested* on a small benchmark (100 samples, 5 seeds, 3 specific models) — present as a strong empirical finding, not a theorem.
2. The social-conformity and round-degradation results are framed by the authors as "preliminary evidence" / "may" / "in many cases." Keep the hedges; do not upgrade "can/sometimes" to "always/will." The claims as written already do this correctly.

No claim was OVERSTATED, UNSUPPORTED, or INACCESSIBLE.
