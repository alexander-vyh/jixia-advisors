# Adversarial Verification — Self-Correction Claims (S5, S6)

Verifier role: hostile fact-checker. Attempted to REFUTE each claim against the primary text.
Date: 2026-06-22.

## Sources

- **Source A** — arXiv:2310.01798v2, "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct
  Reasoning Yet." Huang, Chen, Mishra, Zheng, Yu, Song, Zhou (Google DeepMind &
  UIUC). Submitted 3 Oct 2023, rev. 14 Mar 2024. Full text fetched & indexed
  (HTML v2). ACCESSIBLE.
- **Source B** — TACL, "When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A
  Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs." Kamoi, Y. Zhang, N. Zhang, Han,
  R. Zhang. The MIT Press TACL article page returned **HTTP 403** (hard block),
  but the **identical paper is on arXiv as 2406.01297v3** (TACL camera-ready
  mirror; same title, same authors, same abstract). Abstract fetched & indexed.
  Treated as ACCESSIBLE via the arXiv mirror. (Note: only the abstract +
  metadata were indexed for B; the body sections were not retrieved, so B

## Findings

### A1 — "LLMs cannot reliably self-correct their own reasoning errors using only inherent capabilities (intrinsic self-correction) without external feedback; performance sometimes degrades after self-correction."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**

The claim is a faithful, appropriately-hedged paraphrase of the paper's central
finding. Two independent passages confirm both halves:

- Abstract: *"In the context of reasoning, our research indicates that LLMs
  struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at
  times, their performance even degrades after self-correction."* (Source A
  abstract.) — covers both "cannot reliably ... without external feedback" and
  "performance sometimes degrades."
- Results §3.2: *"We observe that, after self-correction, the accuracies of all
  models drop across all benchmarks."* Table 4 corroborates numerically:
  GPT-4-Turbo GSM8K 91.5 → 88.0 (round 1); Llama-2 GSM8K 62.0 → 43.5 → 36.5;
  Llama-2 CommonSenseQA 64.0 → 37.5 → 36.5. Degradation is real, not rhetorical.

**Refutation attempts that FAILED:**
- *"Degrades" is overstated → maybe it just doesn't improve.* Refuted: Table 4
  shows large drops (Llama-2 CommonSenseQA −26.5 pts over two rounds). The
  claim's hedge ("sometimes degrades") is in fact weaker than the paper's own
  "accuracies of all models drop across all benchmarks."
- *Claim ignores that the paper title says "Yet."* The claim says "cannot
  reliably," which preserves the paper's scope; it does not assert a permanent
  impossibility. No overstatement.

**One scope caveat (does NOT change verdict):** Source A is explicitly scoped to
**reasoning** tasks. Its own §7 Limitations concedes self-correction *does* work
for style/safety alignment. The claim A1 says "reasoning errors," so it stays in
scope. A blanket "LLMs cannot self-correct anything" would be OVERSTATED — but
that is not what A1 claims.

### A2 — "The paper distinguishes intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback) from feedback-grounded correction, defining intrinsic as relying solely on the model's inherent capabilities."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED (verbatim).**

- §2 Background: *"A pivotal distinction lies in the source of feedback ... which
  can purely come from the LLM, or can be drawn from external inputs. Internal
  feedback relies on the model's inherent knowledge and parameters... external
  feedback incorporates inputs from humans, other models, or external tools..."*
- §2: *"our focus shifts to self-correction without any external or human
  feedback. We term this setting **intrinsic self-correction**."*
- Abstract: *"intrinsic self-correction, whereby an LLM attempts to correct its
  initial responses based solely on its inherent capabilities, without the
  crutch of external feedback."*

The phrase "relying solely on the model's inherent capabilities" is an almost
exact quote ("based solely on its inherent capabilities"). The distinction
intrinsic-vs-external is the paper's organizing definition.

**Refutation attempt that FAILED:** *The claim says "feedback-grounded"; the
paper says "external feedback" / "oracle feedback."* These are the same concept
(the paper contrasts intrinsic against oracle-label-guided and tool/human
feedback). Terminological, not substantive — no downgrade.

### B1 — "LLMs cannot reliably self-correct reasoning mistakes using only feedback generated by prompted LLMs; documented successes are confined to tasks exceptionally suited for self-correction."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**

Source B abstract, finding (1) verbatim: *"no prior work demonstrates successful
self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs, except for studies in tasks
that are exceptionally suited for self-correction."*

The claim maps cleanly: "feedback generated by prompted LLMs" = "feedback from
prompted LLMs"; "documented successes confined to tasks exceptionally suited" =
"except for studies in tasks that are exceptionally suited for self-correction."

**Refutation attempts that FAILED:**
- *Claim narrows to "reasoning mistakes"; the survey's finding is general.* The
  survey's finding (1) is not restricted to reasoning, so the claim is actually
  *narrower* than the source — that is under-claiming, not overstating. Still
  SUPPORTED; if anything the source supports a stronger general statement.
- *"Cannot reliably" vs survey's "no prior work demonstrates successful."* The
  survey's phrasing is an evidentiary statement ("no work has shown it") which
  is at least as strong as "cannot reliably." No downgrade.

Minor flag: B1's verdict rests on the abstract's own summary of finding (1)
rather than the survey body (body sections not indexed; only abstract +
metadata). The abstract states the finding as a top-line result, so confidence
remains high, but this is an abstract-level rather than full-body verification.

### B2 — "Self-correction works only under specific enabling conditions: reliable EXTERNAL feedback (oracle/tool/verifier) available, or large-scale fine-tuning trained the behavior in. Internal/intrinsic feedback alone is insufficient."

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.**

Source B abstract findings (2) and (3) verbatim: *"(2) self-correction works
well in tasks that can use reliable external feedback, and (3) large-scale
fine-tuning enables self-correction."* Combined with finding (1) ("no prior work
demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs"
[except exceptionally-suited tasks]), the three findings together state exactly
the claim's structure: the two enabling conditions (reliable external feedback;
large-scale fine-tuning) plus the insufficiency of prompted-LLM/intrinsic
feedback.

Source A independently corroborates the "external feedback works" half: §6
Conclusion details code-execution verifiers, tool use (search/calculator), and
trained verifier/critique models as the route that *does* improve performance.

**Refutation attempts that FAILED:**
- *"Works ONLY under specific conditions" is an absolute the survey doesn't
  make.* Mild tension: the survey says external-feedback tasks work *well* and
  fine-tuning *enables* it, and that prompted-LLM feedback shows no demonstrated
  success **except exceptionally-suited tasks**. So there IS a third sliver
  (exceptionally-suited tasks) where prompted-feedback self-correction is
  documented. B2 folds that exception away into "only ... external feedback or
  fine-tuning." This is a slight over-tightening, but the "exceptionally suited"
  exception is precisely the kind the survey flags as *not* generalizable
  self-correction — so collapsing it does not misrepresent the practical
  takeaway. Verdict stays SUPPORTED rather than OVERSTATED; the nuance is logged.
- *"Internal feedback alone is insufficient" — does the survey say that?* Yes by
  construction: finding (1) says prompted-LLM (internal) feedback shows no
  demonstrated general success. Supported.
