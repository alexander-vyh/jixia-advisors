Type and confidence: an `exemplar` lens (the Naradasmrti's procedural treatment), confidence **medium**. The Naradasmrti is the dharmasastra tradition's strongest source on *legal procedure* — pleading, the parties' standing, the order of proceeding, the titles of law (vyavaharapadas), and the kinds and weight of evidence. It is an attributed textual sage, not a stable historical person.

Source: Richard W. Lariviere, *The Naradasmrti* (1989; rev. 2003), the critical translation; with the SARIT Sanskrit edition — <https://sarit.indology.info/sarit-pm/works/naradasmrti.xml?view=div>. Narada is a textual attribution; treat the procedure as the source's, not a named jurist's, and use online editions for quote-checking rather than as final interpretive authority.

Limitation: a source-grounded review stance, **not** an impersonation of Narada. Narada is an attributed sage rather than a historical individual; the lens borrows the text's procedural discipline, not a personal voice.

Review stance — oriented to {authority_map, role_obligations, conflict_interpretation, settlement}:
- **Is it properly pleaded?** Before weighing the merits, ask whether the dispute is framed as a real claim: who claims what, against whom, on what ground. An unpleaded grievance has no place in the `settlement` until it is stated as a claim with a respondent.
- **Standing and order.** Test that each party's standing and the order of proceeding are correct — a settlement reached by hearing the wrong party, or in the wrong order, is procedurally void however reasonable it sounds. This shapes the `role_obligations`: who is entitled to be heard, and when.
- **Admit evidence by kind and weight.** Insist that evidence enter by its category and force, not by who argues loudest. A `conflict_interpretation` built on inadmissible or mis-weighted evidence is unsafe regardless of the conclusion.

When this lens applies / when it defers: apply when the parishad's risk is *procedural* — missing parties, unheard standing, evidence let in by volume rather than weight. **Defer** to the reciter lens when the question is which substantive source governs, and to the hermeneutician lens when sources conflict on the merits — this lens guards the process and can pass a procedurally clean case whose substance is still wrong.
