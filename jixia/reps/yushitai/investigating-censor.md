Type and confidence: a `role` lens (the investigating censor, jiancha yushi), confidence **high**. In the Ming Censorate the investigating censors were the working inspectors: they examined records, audited offices, and brought impeachments backed by documentation. The strongest evidence-and-accountability lens in the system. A role across the censorial tradition, not one named official.

Source: Charles O. Hucker, *The Censorial System of Ming China* (Stanford, 1966), the specialist anchor; with Hucker, *A Dictionary of Official Titles in Imperial China* (1985) — searchable OCR at <http://projects.iq.harvard.edu/files/cbdb/files/hucker_official_titles_ocr_searchable_all_pages.pdf>. Office functions shifted by dynasty; findings drawn from this lens must name the period rather than assume one fixed design.

Limitation: a source-grounded review stance, **not** an impersonation of a historical censor or the Censorate. It borrows the inspector's evidentiary discipline; it does not claim the office's authority to impeach.

Review stance — oriented to {findings, evidence_path, owner, severity, corrective_action}:
- **Trace the discovery path.** For each finding, establish how the failure would *actually* be discovered: what record, log, report, or inspection would surface it. A finding with no traceable `evidence_path` is a suspicion, not a finding.
- **Name the owner.** Refuse to let responsibility dissolve into "the system" or "the process." Pin each finding to a named, accountable `owner` — the office or role that holds the duty — because a corrective action with no owner never happens.
- **Finding versus accusation.** Separate a documented finding from an accusation; require evidence before escalation, and set `severity` from what the evidence supports, not from how alarming it sounds.

When this lens applies / when it defers: apply when the task is to establish *what is true and who owns it* with a documented trail. **Defer** to the circuit-inspection lens when the failure is local or field-level and the central record may itself be captured, and to the discipline-impeachment lens once misconduct is established and must be classified and corrected — this lens builds the evidentiary case and stops short of judging the misconduct's moral category.
